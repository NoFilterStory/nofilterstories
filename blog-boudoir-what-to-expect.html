What to Expect at a Boudoir Photo Shoot

By Geo June 28, 2026 7 min read

Quick answer A boudoir session runs 2–3 hours. You bring 2–4 outfits, the photographer guides every pose, and the only thing you actually have to do is show up. Most clients feel nervous for the first ten minutes and completely at ease by the end. You'll see your edited gallery within 2–3 weeks.

The number one thing clients tell me before a boudoir session is: "I'm not sure I can do this." The number one thing they tell me after: "I wish I'd done this years ago." Here's exactly what happens at a No Filters Stories boudoir shoot, from the moment you arrive to the moment you leave.

Before the Shoot: What to Prepare

The week before your session, I'll send you a prep guide. The short version: stay hydrated, skip heavy-waistband clothing for a few hours before you arrive (elastic marks take 20–30 minutes to fade), and bring 2–4 outfits that make you feel like yourself — or the version of yourself you want to see in photos.

What photographs best? Lacy lingerie sets, silky robes, oversized button-down shirts, bodysuits. What doesn't matter? The size on the tag. Bring what makes you feel something, not what you think "looks good in photos." I'll handle the rest.

Outfit tip: Bring one outfit that's comfortable and familiar — maybe something you already own — and one that feels a little bolder than usual. The session is a safe space to try both.

When You Arrive

We start slow. Music, a drink if you want one, and a conversation — not a briefing. I want to know how you're feeling, what you're hoping to take away from the session, and anything that might make you more comfortable.

If you've booked hair and makeup, that happens first and takes about 60–90 minutes. This time is actually valuable beyond the obvious: by the time you sit down in front of the camera, you've been in the space for over an hour and it doesn't feel strange anymore.

During the Shoot: What Actually Happens

I direct everything. You won't be left to figure out what to do with your hands. Every pose is guided — where to put your chin, how to shift your weight, when to look at the camera and when to look away. A big part of my job is reading how you're feeling in real time and adjusting accordingly.

We'll typically work through 3–4 different setups over the course of 2–3 hours. Each setup has its own mood: some will feel more playful, others more intimate. You set the tone; I follow your lead on how far you want to push it.

Most clients hit a turning point around 20–30 minutes in. The self-consciousness fades and something more natural takes over. That's when the best photos happen.

What to Do If You Feel Nervous

Feel it. Don't try to perform confidence you don't have yet — nervous energy shows in photos in a way that's often more interesting than forced confidence anyway. If something isn't working, say so. If you want to take a break, take one. This is your session.

First-time boudoir clients are actually my favourite to photograph, because there's always a genuine moment of surprise — the first time they see themselves in the back of the camera and think "wait, that's me?"

After the Shoot: Your Photos

Your edited gallery is typically ready within 2–3 weeks. I'll send you a private link and we'll go through the images together if you'd like. From there, you choose what you want printed, downloaded, or kept in an album.

One thing I tell every client: don't edit the photos yourself after delivery. Not because the images are locked — they're yours — but because the editing decisions are part of the artistic result. Trust them for a week before you decide how you feel.

Common Questions About Boudoir Sessions

What should I wear to a boudoir photo shoot?

Bring 2–4 outfits that make you feel confident — lingerie, a silky robe, an oversized button-down, or something personally meaningful. Avoid anything with tight waistbands that leave marks on your skin, since those take 20–30 minutes to fade.

Do I need to be a certain size or body type?

No. Boudoir photography is for every body. My job is to find the light, angles, and poses that celebrate exactly how you look right now — not some imagined future version of you. Clients of all sizes, ages, and shapes consistently leave surprised by how much they love their photos.

Will I feel comfortable during a boudoir session?

Most clients say they felt nervous for the first ten minutes and completely at ease by the end. Sessions start with conversation and music you choose — there's no rushing, and every pose is guided throughout.

How long does a boudoir photo shoot take?

Most sessions run 2–3 hours. Hair and makeup, if included, adds 60–90 minutes before the shoot begins.

When will I see my photos?

Your edited gallery is typically ready within 2–3 weeks of your session.

Is boudoir photography only for women?

Not at all. Boudoir sessions are for anyone who wants intimate, confidence-celebrating portraits — women, men, non-binary individuals, and couples all book.
